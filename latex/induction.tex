Prove that $(1 + x)^{21{,}127} \geq 1 + 21{,}127\,x$ for all real number $x \geq -1$.

\newpage
\showsolution{
  We will prove that for all real $x \geq -1$ and any integer $n \geq 1$,
  $$(1+x)^n \geq 1 + n x$$

  Let $x \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $x \geq -1$. We proceed by induction on $n \geq 1$.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base Case:} Let $n = 1$. Then
      $
      (1+x)^1 = 1 + x = 1 + 1 \cdot x,
      $
      so the inequality holds.
      \vspace{10pt}

    \item \textbf{Inductive Case:} Let $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $n \geq 1$.

    \item \textbf{Inductive Hypothesis:} Assume that
      $(1+x)^n \geq 1 + n x$.

    \item \textbf{Inductive Step:} Consider $n+1$. Then
      $$
      (1+x)^{n+1} = (1+x)^n (1+x).
      $$
      Using the inductive hypothesis, we have
      $$
      (1+x)^{n+1} \geq (1 + n x)(1 + x) = 1 + (n+1)x + n x^2.
      $$
      Since $x^2 \geq 0$, it follows that
      $$
      (1+x)^{n+1} \geq 1 + (n+1)x.
      $$
  \end{itemize}

  By the Principle of Mathematical Induction, we conclude that for all integers $n \geq 1$
  $$
  (1+x)^n \geq 1 + n x
  $$
}
